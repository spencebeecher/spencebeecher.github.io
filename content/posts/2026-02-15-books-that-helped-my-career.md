---

Data science asks for two different kinds of skill. There is the technical half: programming (Python is usually enough), statistics, probability and machine learning. Then there is the half that decides whether any of it gets used — leading a team, making a case with data, and explaining a result to someone who will act on it. I would note that even if you aren't a manager, you should understand management.

Here is what helped me, hard skills first.

## Statistical Foundations

### 1. [All of Statistics](https://www.amazon.com/All-Statistics-Statistical-Inference-Springer/dp/0387402721) by Larry Wasserman

Statistical theory in one short, rigorous volume. It is the book that fills in the maths under everything else on this list.

### 2. [The Elements of Statistical Learning](https://www.amazon.com/Elements-Statistical-Learning-Prediction-Statistics/dp/0387848576) by Trevor Hastie, Robert Tibshirani, and Jerome Friedman

The reference for statistical learning methods. Dense — I use it to look things up rather than to read straight through.

### 3. [100 Statistical Tests](https://www.amazon.com/100-Statistical-Tests-Gopal-Kanji/dp/1849203539) by Gopal K. Kanji

A lookup table for picking the right test. Where I go when designing an experiment or reading an A/B test.

### 4. [The Book of Why: The New Science of Cause and Effect](https://www.amazon.com/Book-Why-Science-Cause-Effect/dp/046509760X) by Judea Pearl and Dana Mackenzie

Pearl gives you a way to say what causes what, rather than what moves with what. It changed how I think about experiments. For the technical treatment, see his earlier [Causality](https://www.amazon.com/Causality-Reasoning-Inference-Judea-Pearl/dp/052189560X). Also worth reading: [Data Analysis Using Regression and Multilevel/Hierarchical Models](https://www.amazon.com/Analysis-Regression-Multilevel-Hierarchical-Models/dp/052168689X) by Gelman and Hill.

## Machine Learning & Big Data

### 5. [Mining of Massive Datasets](http://www.mmds.org/) by Jure Leskovec, Anand Rajaraman, and Jeff Ullman

Algorithms and systems for data that does not fit on one machine. Free online.

### 6. [Machine Learning Course](https://www.coursera.org/learn/machine-learning) by Andrew Ng

Still the clearest introduction I know of, and the exercises are the part that teaches.

### 7. [Linear Algebra Course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/) by Gilbert Strang (MIT OpenCourseWare)

Linear algebra is the language machine learning is written in. Strang shows you what the operations are doing, not just how to do them.

## Leadership & Team Building

### 8. [The Carrot Principle](https://www.amazon.com/Carrot-Principle-Managers-Extraordinary-Results/dp/0743297261) by Adrian Gostick and Chester Elton

Recognition, treated as a tool with a method rather than a nice gesture.

### 9. [High Output Management](https://www.amazon.com/High-Output-Management-Andrew-Grove/dp/0679762884) by Andrew Grove

Grove's argument is that a manager's output is the output of their team, so the job is finding the actions with the most leverage. That transfers directly to running a data science team.

## Communication & Influence

### 10. [Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion](https://www.amazon.com/Influence-Psychology-Persuasion-Robert-Cialdini/dp/006124189X) by Robert Cialdini

An analysis is worth nothing until someone acts on it. Cialdini is about how a recommendation gets framed so that happens.

### 11. [The Visual Display of Quantitative Information](https://www.amazon.com/Visual-Display-Quantitative-Information/dp/1930824130) by Edward Tufte

This is a great coffee table book that gets you thinking about visualization. I would note though that often the most impactful visualizations are quite simple and accompanied with clear calls to action.

### 12. [How to Know a Person](https://www.amazon.com/How-Know-Person-Seeing-Others/dp/0593652908) by David Brooks

Brooks is on listening, and on what it takes to actually understand the person across from you. Most of leading people runs through that.

## Innovation & Technology

### 13. [The Innovator's Dilemma](https://www.amazon.com/Innovators-Dilemma-Technologies-Management-Innovation/dp/1633691780) by Clayton Christensen

Why good companies lose to worse products, and how to place a data science bet inside one.

### 14. [The Soul of a New Machine](https://www.amazon.com/Soul-New-Machine-Tracy-Kidder/dp/0316491977) by Tracy Kidder

Kidder followed a team building a computer in the late 1970s. It won a Pulitzer, and it is the best description I have read of what engineering work actually feels like.

## Critical Thinking

### 15. [Justice Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kBdfcR-8hEY&list=PL30C13C91CFFEFEA6) by Michael Sandel (Harvard)

The systems we build make decisions about people. Sandel is where I go for how to think about the ones that are not technical questions at all.
